Notice it. Let it go.How to let go of negative thoughts

Letting go of a negative thought doesn’t mean forcing it out. It means you stop carrying it. Shift is the second step of the Lumanta method: you acknowledge the thought without judgment, analysis or attachment, and let it pass.

Quick answer

Letting go, in short

  • You don’t have to fight or argue with the thought.
  • Acknowledge it, then stop following it.
  • Let it pass, like a cloud crossing the sky.
  • If it comes back, notice it again. That’s still the practice.

Shift comes right after you catch a thought. It’s the moment of release that makes room for what comes next.

What happens when you fight a negative thought?

When a thought hurts, the instinct is to push it away. But pushing is still a way of holding on. Arguing with the thought, digging into where it came from or scolding yourself for thinking it all keep you in contact with it. The thought gets more of your attention, not less.

Shift works differently. You’ve already caught the thought. Now you simply decline to follow it.

How to let go of negative thoughts: a gentle release

Try this the next time you catch a thought. It takes about as long as a few slow breaths.

  1. Acknowledge it.“That’s a judgmental thought.” Say it silently, without commentary.
  2. Breathe out slowly.Let your exhale be a little longer than your inhale. It’s a natural moment of release.
  3. Let it pass.Picture the thought drifting by like a cloud, or a leaf carried down a stream. You don’t have to push it. Just stop holding it.
  4. Come back to now.Notice your feet, your breath, the room around you. If the thought returns, notice it again.

The cloud image is a common one in mindfulness. A self-help guide from UNC counseling services describes thoughts as clouds passing through the sky of your awareness.

How to let go of negative thoughts in everyday moments

Letting go isn’t only for quiet moments on a cushion. Here’s what Shift can look like in a real day.

  • After a sharp emailNotice the judgment, breathe out, and let it pass before you reply.
  • Lying awake at nightCatch the worry, name it once, and let it drift. Then come back to your breath.
  • After a disagreementNotice the replay in your head. You don’t have to win it again.
  • Scrolling your phoneCatch the comparison as it shows up. Let it go and put the phone down.
  • Before a big momentNotice the fear, acknowledge it kindly, and let it pass through.
  • Stuck in trafficCatch the frustration. Let it pass. Notice what’s around you.

Each one is the same small move: notice it, let it go, and come back to now.

How do you let go of negative thoughts spiritually?

Many spiritual traditions teach some form of non-attachment: holding life, and the mind, a little more loosely. Letting go of a thought isn’t pretending it never happened, and it isn’t avoiding what it might be pointing to. It’s choosing not to carry it into your next moment.

You might pair Shift with something from your own practice: a breath, a short prayer, a mantra, a moment of stillness. Lumanta doesn’t ask you to believe anything. It simply gives the release a place to happen.

What if the same negative thought keeps coming back?

It probably will, at least for a while. Thoughts that have run for years tend to return. That isn’t failure. Each time you notice and release a returning thought, you’re practicing.

There’s nothing to perfect here. You don’t have to catch every thought or release it perfectly. Keep noticing. Keep releasing.

You don’t have to carry that thought.
